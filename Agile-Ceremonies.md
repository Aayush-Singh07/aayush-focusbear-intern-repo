# Agile Ceremonies & Team Collaboration — Aayush Kumar Singh

## Research & Learn

**Main Agile ceremonies and their purpose:**
- **Daily Stand-ups:** short, frequent check-ins (what I did, what's next, any blockers) to keep the team aligned without needing a long meeting.
- **Sprint Planning (Scrum) vs. Continuous Prioritization (Kanban):** Scrum locks in a batch of work at the start of a sprint; Kanban continuously reprioritizes the backlog as new information arrives, pulling in the next-highest-priority item whenever capacity opens up.
- **Retrospectives:** a regular look-back at what went well, what didn't, and what to change — focused on improving the team's process, not just the product.
- **Backlog Refinement:** ongoing review and clarification of upcoming work so tickets are well-defined and ready to be picked up, rather than vague or under-specified when someone finally starts them.

**How Agile teams collaborate asynchronously and across time zones:**
Given Focus Bear's team spans Vietnam, Philippines, Ethiopia, India, and Melbourne, most collaboration has to work without everyone online simultaneously — written, detailed async updates (PR descriptions, issue comments, standup notes) matter more than live conversation, since a teammate might only see your update hours after you post it. Clear, self-contained written communication reduces the number of back-and-forth round trips needed across time zones, which is the real cost of async work.

## Reflection

**How Agile ceremonies help with communication and alignment:**
They create regular, predictable checkpoints for surfacing blockers and misalignment early, instead of everyone silently working in their own direction and only discovering a mismatch once something's already built wrong.

**Which ceremony is most important for my role, and why:**
Daily standups, specifically because I'm async and remote across a large time-zone gap — a short, clear standup update is often the only real-time signal my team gets from me each day, so making it genuinely useful (not just "worked on stuff") matters more for me than it might for someone sitting in the same office as their team.
