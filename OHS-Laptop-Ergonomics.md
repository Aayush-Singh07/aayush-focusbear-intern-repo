## Research & Learn

**Risks of using a laptop without an external monitor or keyboard:**
Laptop screens sit too low for a naturally upright neck position, forcing users to look down for extended periods — leading to neck and upper back strain over time. The attached keyboard and trackpad also force wrists and shoulders into a cramped, hunched position since screen and keyboard height can't be adjusted independently.

**Ergonomic equipment that can improve posture:**
- Laptop stand (raises screen to eye level)
- External keyboard and mouse (allows the laptop to sit higher while keeping hands at a natural, relaxed height)
- Ergonomic chair with proper lumbar support, or a cushion/towel roll as a low-cost alternative
- External monitor, if available, for better screen positioning and reduced eye strain

**Adjustments for monitor height, chair position, and desk setup:**
- Top of the screen should be roughly at or just below eye level, so the neck stays neutral rather than tilted down
- Chair height should allow feet flat on the floor (or on a footrest) with knees roughly at hip height
- Elbows should stay close to the body at roughly a 90-degree angle while typing
- Screen distance roughly an arm's length away to reduce eye strain

**Daily habits that reduce the impact of prolonged laptop use:**
- Taking short movement breaks every 30-60 minutes rather than sitting still for hours straight
- Doing periodic posture checks — consciously resetting shoulders back and down, chin level
- Following the 20-20-20 rule for eye strain: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds
- Standing or stretching briefly between focus sessions rather than only at the end of the day

## Reflection

**Equipment changes to improve my workspace setup:**
Currently working primarily on a laptop directly — the most impactful low-cost change is propping the laptop up (books or a stand) and using an external keyboard/mouse where possible, rather than typing directly on the laptop at desk height.

**Behavioural changes to improve posture and reduce strain:**
Consciously sitting upright rather than slouching forward toward the screen, taking a short break to stand/stretch between each focused work block instead of only at the very end of a session, and periodically checking screen height/distance rather than settling into whatever position I happened to start in.

**Reminders to maintain posture and take breaks:**
Using Focus Bear's own built-in movement/break reminders during work sessions — since I'll be actively using the app as part of logging my internship hours, this doubles as a practical way to build the habit rather than relying on willpower alone to remember.

## Task

**Adjustment made:** Raised laptop to closer to eye level using available books as a stand, and set a periodic reminder (via Focus Bear) to check posture and take short movement breaks during work sessions.

**Equipment identified as a future improvement:** An external keyboard would meaningfully improve wrist position compared to typing directly on the laptop — noting this as a next equipment upgrade to make.

**One-day trial note:** Using scheduled movement/posture reminders during a full work session noticeably reduced the tendency to stay hunched forward for long uninterrupted stretches — the biggest benefit wasn't any single break, but simply being interrupted often enough to reset posture before strain built up.
