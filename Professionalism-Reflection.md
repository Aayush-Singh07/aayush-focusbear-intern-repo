# Professionalism Reflection — Aayush Kumar Singh

## Research & Learn

**Professional vs. unprofessional behaviour in a casual work environment:**
- *Professional:* showing up on time to meetings, giving clear async updates, responding to messages within a reasonable window, admitting mistakes directly instead of hiding them, staying respectful even in disagreement
- *Unprofessional:* going silent without explanation, dismissing feedback defensively, gossiping about teammates, taking credit for others' work, being sloppy with commitments (saying yes to a deadline and then missing it without communication)
- A casual/fun culture doesn't lower the bar on reliability and respect — if anything, it relies more on people self-managing well since there's less rigid structure enforcing it

**Respectful communication in remote teams:**
- Tone is easy to misread in text — err on the side of clarity and warmth rather than assuming the reader will interpret a short message charitably
- Handling disagreements: focus on the specific issue/decision, not the person; ask questions before assuming bad intent
- Responding to feedback: acknowledge it, ask clarifying questions if needed, avoid getting defensive even if the first reaction is discomfort

**Giving and receiving feedback professionally:**
- Focus on actions and outcomes, not personal traits ("this function has a bug" not "you're careless")
- Assume good intent from the other person, especially in async text where tone can be misread
- Ask for clarification instead of reacting defensively if feedback feels unclear or unexpected
- When giving feedback, be specific enough that the other person knows exactly what to change

**Guide for working with supervisors (reviewed):**
Key takeaways: come prepared to check-ins with clear updates and specific questions rather than vague status reports, be proactive about flagging blockers early rather than waiting to be asked, and treat supervisor time as valuable — batch smaller questions rather than interrupting repeatedly for minor things where reasonable.

## Reflection

**Experiencing or witnessing great teamwork and professionalism:**
In my experience leading a 50+ person team as Head Coordinator for my college's department tech fest, the moments that worked best were when people communicated blockers early instead of quietly struggling, and when feedback during planning was direct but never personal — that combination of honesty and respect kept the whole event running smoothly under real time pressure.

**Steps to keep communication clear, respectful, and inclusive:**
Being explicit rather than assuming shared context, keeping messages concise and scannable, and checking in on tone before sending anything that could be misread in text.

**Helping create a positive, fun, professional environment:**
Being reliable (doing what I say I'll do), giving credit where it's due, and keeping a light tone in casual conversation while staying focused and dependable on actual deliverables.

**Preparing effectively for meetings with supervisors/colleagues:**
Reviewing what's been done since the last check-in, having specific questions ready rather than open-ended ones, and flagging blockers in advance so the meeting time is used efficiently rather than spent figuring out what to even discuss.

**What being proactive during this internship looks like:**
Not waiting to be told the next step when it's reasonably clear from the milestone/issue structure, flagging confusion or blockers early rather than sitting on them, and asking good clarifying questions instead of guessing silently when something's genuinely ambiguous.

**Following up and escalating:**
First follow-up: a polite, direct async message referencing the original ask, giving a specific timeframe if relevant. If there's no response after a reasonable window (a few days, depending on urgency), a gentle second follow-up. If something is genuinely blocking and time-sensitive with no response after that, escalate to a team lead or the contact provided for major issues — but only after a real, good-faith attempt to resolve it directly first.

**Things I won't do:**
I won't go silent on a commitment without communicating, I won't take credit for work that isn't mine, I won't let a disagreement turn personal, and I won't sit on a blocker for days hoping it resolves itself instead of flagging it.
