## Research & Learn

**How a Kanban board works, and how it helps manage workflow:**
A Kanban board is a visual representation of work as it moves through stages, using cards (tasks/issues) placed in columns. It helps because anyone can glance at the board and instantly see what's in progress, what's blocked, and what's ready for review — without needing a status meeting to find out. It makes bottlenecks visible (e.g., a column filling up faster than it empties) rather than hidden in individual to-do lists.

**What the columns typically represent:**
- **Backlog / Not Started:** work identified but not yet begun
- **In Progress:** actively being worked on
- **Blocked:** stalled on a dependency, question, or external factor
- **Ready for Review:** work completed by the doer, awaiting review/approval
- **Done:** fully completed and merged/shipped

**How tasks move through the board, and who updates them:**
The person actively working the task is responsible for moving their own card as its status changes — pulling it into "In Progress" when starting, moving to "Ready for Review" once pushed, and only marking "Done" after review/approval. This keeps the board accurate in real time rather than relying on someone else to track it for you.

**Benefits of limiting work in progress (WIP):**
Limiting WIP forces focus — instead of half-finishing five things at once, capping how much can be "in progress" at a time keeps attention on actually finishing tasks rather than just starting them. It also surfaces bottlenecks faster: if the WIP limit is constantly maxed out in one column, that's a clear, visible signal something needs attention.

## Reflection

**How Kanban helps manage priorities and avoid overload:**
Because the board makes current workload visible, it's harder to quietly overcommit — a full "In Progress" column is an obvious signal to finish something before starting anything new, rather than an invisible pile-up that only becomes obvious once deadlines are missed.

**Improving my own workflow using Kanban principles:**
Being disciplined about only pulling in one task at a time rather than opening multiple issues "to make progress on all of them," and moving my own cards promptly and honestly (not leaving something in "In Progress" long after it's actually blocked) so the board stays a trustworthy source of truth.
