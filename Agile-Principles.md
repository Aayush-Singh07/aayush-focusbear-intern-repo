## Research & Learn

**What Agile is, and how it differs from traditional project management:**
Agile is an iterative approach to building software — work is broken into small increments, delivered frequently, and adjusted based on continuous feedback. Traditional ("waterfall") project management plans everything upfront in sequential phases (requirements → design → build → test → release) and only sees a working product at the very end. Agile instead ships small, working pieces continuously, so mistakes and changing priorities get caught early instead of after months of work.

**Core values and principles of Agile (Agile Manifesto):**
- Individuals and interactions over processes and tools
- Working software over comprehensive documentation
- Customer collaboration over contract negotiation
- Responding to change over following a fixed plan
The underlying idea: rigid upfront planning and heavy documentation matter less than actually shipping something useful and adapting fast when reality doesn't match the plan.

**Scrum vs. Kanban, and when each is used:**
- **Scrum** works in fixed-length sprints (e.g., 2 weeks), with defined roles (Scrum Master, Product Owner, Dev Team), sprint planning, and a sprint backlog that's locked once the sprint starts. Good for teams that benefit from predictable, structured cycles.
- **Kanban** is continuous — there are no fixed sprints, work flows through a board (To Do → In Progress → Review → Done) and new work gets pulled in as capacity allows, based on WIP (work-in-progress) limits. Better suited for teams with unpredictable or continuously arriving work.

**Why Focus Bear leans towards Kanban instead of Scrum:**
Focus Bear is a small, globally distributed team (members across Vietnam, Philippines, Ethiopia, India, Melbourne) working across many time zones with interns joining/leaving on different, overlapping timelines. Fixed sprint cycles assume everyone is available together at consistent intervals, which doesn't fit an async, rolling-onboarding team well. Kanban's continuous-flow model fits better — work moves forward whenever someone has capacity, without needing the whole team synchronized to a sprint calendar.

## Reflection

**Biggest benefits and challenges of Agile:**
The biggest benefit is that problems surface early — shipping small pieces means a wrong assumption gets caught in days, not months. The biggest challenge is that it requires real discipline in communication; without a heavy upfront plan to fall back on, the team has to stay genuinely aligned through frequent, clear updates instead.

**How Agile principles can improve my role:**
As an intern working async across a big time-zone gap, the "responding to change" and "working software over documentation" principles matter most directly — I should prioritize getting a real, working feature/fix in front of the team quickly rather than over-planning or over-documenting before writing any code.

## Task

**Scrum vs. Kanban, in my own words:**
Scrum is like planning a fixed two-week sprint of work and committing to finish exactly that before starting anything new. Kanban is more like a continuous conveyor belt — you pull in the next task whenever you have room, without waiting for a new "sprint" to officially start. Scrum suits teams that sync up together often; Kanban suits teams (like Focus Bear) that are spread across time zones and need work to keep flowing without everyone being online at once.

**One Agile principle I think will be most useful in my work:**
"Working software over comprehensive documentation" — given I'm still building confidence in this codebase, I'll get more value out of shipping small, real, working changes and getting fast feedback than out of over-planning a feature in detail before writing any code.
